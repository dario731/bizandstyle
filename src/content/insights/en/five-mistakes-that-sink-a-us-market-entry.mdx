## The pattern behind most failures

Companies rarely fail in the United States because the product is wrong. They fail because the entry was organised as a series of trips, a trade show and a distributor contract, with nobody accountable in the market between one visit and the next.

Five mistakes come up again and again.

## 1. Advising from home

A consultant in Milan, Madrid or São Paulo can write an excellent plan. They cannot answer a buyer's question on a Tuesday afternoon in Miami. Market entry is executed where the market is.

## 2. Confusing a study with an action

Feasibility studies are useful. They are also a comfortable place to stop. If the plan does not name who will be on the ground, with which budget, for how long, it is a document, not a decision.

## 3. Arriving without infrastructure

No warehouse, no logistics partner, no bank account, no legal setup. Every first order then becomes an improvisation, and the customer notices.

## 4. Underestimating compliance

Customs classification, labelling, tax registration, immigration for the people you send. Each one is manageable. Together, handled late, they delay the launch by quarters.

## 5. Arriving as an unknown

Credibility in the U.S. is built by being present, repeatedly. A company that appears once a year at a fair is a supplier. A company with a local executive, a local address and a local network is a partner.

## What the alternative looks like

The companies that succeed treat the United States as a market to belong to, not a country to sell into. They put a senior person in Miami, plug into infrastructure that already exists, and return to the market on a schedule. That is the difference between export and internationalization, and it is the whole reason BIZ & STYLE is organised the way it is.
