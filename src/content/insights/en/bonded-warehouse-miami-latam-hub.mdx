A brand entering the Americas usually faces the same question twice: where do we hold inventory for the United States, and where do we hold it for Latin America and the Caribbean? Two answers mean two warehouses, two customs regimes and two sets of working capital tied up in duty.

A Miami bonded warehouse gives one answer.

## How bonded storage works

Goods arriving in bond are held without paying U.S. duty or tax. Duty is due only when, and if, goods are released into the U.S. market. Goods re-exported to Latin America or the Caribbean leave without ever paying U.S. import duty. While in bond, goods can be stored, sorted, labelled and repackaged.

## Why that matters for LATAM

Miami sits on the routes: PortMiami is the cargo gateway of the Americas, and MIA is one of the world's busiest airports. Holding regional inventory here means shipping to the U.S. when demand justifies it, and distributing across Latin America duty-efficiently from the same pallets — with consolidation and flexibility that a distributor's warehouse in a single country cannot offer.

## Domestic and bonded, side by side

Bonded storage is only half of the answer. Once goods are cleared for the U.S., they need domestic storage, B2B and B2C fulfillment, marketplace-ready labelling and returns handling. Operating both regimes in one Miami facility is what lets a brand run the U.S. and LATAM as one operation instead of two.
